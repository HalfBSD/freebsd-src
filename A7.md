A7. Frame Relay and obsolete synchronous-WAN Netgraph protocols
Purpose and locations: Remove Netgraph support specifically for legacy Frame Relay and closely associated synchronous-WAN protocols, including ng_frame_relay*, ng_lmi*, ng_rfc1490*, and ng_cisco*, together with their dedicated headers, kernel modules, build entries, documentation, tests, and examples.
FreeBSD rationale / HalfBSD fit: These facilities support Frame Relay, LMI signaling, RFC1490-style Frame Relay encapsulation, and Cisco HDLC on synchronous WAN links. They are useful for legacy telecom, router, and network-appliance environments, but have no identified role in HalfBSD's workstation, ordinary Ethernet/IP networking, VM/NAT, or experimental Netgraph use.
Dependencies: Treat these as protocol-specific leaf nodes. Do not remove or simplify generic Netgraph infrastructure merely because these nodes are removed. I want Netgraph as a programmable networking playground, but i don't need Netgraph to speak to a 1997 telco. Audit reverse dependencies before deletion and preserve any shared Netgraph APIs or infrastructure used by retained nodes.
Disposition and impact: Delete the identified legacy WAN nodes and their dedicated integration. Loss should be limited to the ability to construct those specific Frame Relay/Cisco-HDLC Netgraph graphs.
Benefit / risk / confidence: Modest source and module reduction, but meaningful removal of obsolete protocol surface. Low expected risk; high confidence for Frame Relay/LMI/RFC1490 and Cisco HDLC, subject to dependency verification.
Validation: Build retained Netgraph modules and userland tools; verify creation/use of representative generic Netgraph nodes; verify retained bhyve networking paths; confirm no remaining references to the removed protocol nodes.
